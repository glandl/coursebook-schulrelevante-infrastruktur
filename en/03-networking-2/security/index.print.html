Security and data protection

No legal advice

This section gives technical orientation. Legal questions (e.g. logging, retention periods, youth protection filters) are clarified by the school with the data protection officer and the school maintainer.

The essentials

TopicMeasure
A VLAN is not a firewallAlways apply access rules between VLANs according to the permission matrix. Without rules, every VLAN reaches every other one via the router.
Native VLANOn trunks, do not use VLAN 1 as the untagged VLAN; use a separate, otherwise unused VLAN. This makes VLAN hopping harder (an attacker sends frames with two tags).
Unused portsShut them down (shutdown) and assign them to an unused VLAN. Network sockets in classrooms are freely accessible.
No automatic trunksConfigure ports to end devices explicitly as access ports (switchport mode access) so that no device can negotiate a trunk.
Rogue DHCP serversDHCP snooping on the switch: DHCP replies only from the ports of the real servers.
Network managementManage switches, access points and routers only from the management VLAN (99), never from the student or guest Wi-Fi.
DNS resolverAnswer recursive queries only from your own network, otherwise the server can be abused for attacks on third parties.
Guest Wi-FiSeparate VLAN, internet only, devices isolated from each other. A captive portal encrypts nothing.

Data protection in the school context

  • DHCP logs show which device (MAC address, device name such as “Annas-Laptop”) was on the network when. That is personal data, students’ included.
  • DNS logs show which sites were visited. They reveal more than many people think. Decide whether to log, who may see the logs and when they are deleted.
  • DNS filters (e.g. for youth protection) are common. They should be transparent: students and parents should know that filtering happens and by which rules.
  • Captive portals with registration (name, e-mail, phone number) collect data. Ask only for what is necessary and delete the data after a short time.
